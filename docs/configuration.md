# Trading terminal configuration

The trading terminal shell is in `public/app.html`; presentation styles and behavior live in `public/css/app.css` and `public/js/app.js`. Market options and initial plan defaults are intentionally kept outside the HTML:

- Edit `public/config/markets.json` to add pairs, choose TradingView symbols, group menu items, and select a quote source. Set `"enabled": false` to hide a pair from new trades while retaining quote support for existing positions.
- `config/plans.json` supplies initial defaults during a one-time seed, inserting missing named plans without overwriting existing rows. Admins manage persisted plans in Admin Control: list price, capital, profit target, DLL/MLL, consistency limit, and active-for-sale state. The standard discount determines the current sale price. Deactivating a plan removes it from new purchases without deleting its history or changing accounts that reference it; database edits and new plans are not overwritten or retired at startup.

The existing plan definitions use a 5% DLL calculated from the current daily starting balance and a 10% MLL loss amount fixed from initial capital. At each UTC day rollover, the DLL starting balance resets to the prior day's closing equity. The MLL loss amount stays fixed, while its high-water mark trails up from end-of-day equity and is capped at initial capital plus that loss amount. For a $5,000 account, the $500 MLL trail therefore stops at $5,500 and its breach floor stops at $5,000. Profit target/progress remains calculated from initial capital. A DLL breach locks the account for 24 hours; an MLL breach permanently locks it. A server-side UTC rollover runs every minute and saves daily balance/profit summaries for the account chart; account detail/trade requests also catch up a missed rollover.

Accounts display as compact cards with capital, balance, profit, and risk previews. The Accounts view shows active accounts by default; turn off “Show active accounts only” to review breached, frozen, or withdrawal-paused accounts. Expand a card to see the daily profit graph and trade history. The graph refreshes along with account data while the Accounts view is open.

Account payouts are available only from balance above the MLL trail cap (initial capital plus the plan's configured MLL loss amount). For example, a $5,000 account with a 10% MLL has a $5,500 protected payout balance. At $5,600, up to $100 gross can be requested. The request does not change the balance until approval; on approval the full gross is deducted, 90% is the user's transfer, and 10% is the administrator's share. Requests are reserved against the available excess while pending. A pending account payout automatically pauses new trades until the request is approved or rejected; this automatic pause is independent from a manual administrator freeze and the DLL/MLL locks. Admins search users by username or wallet to inspect their live and archived account balances and trade history, approve or reject requests, freeze/unfreeze individual accounts, and suspend/restore user-wide account access. A user suspension preserves wallet sign-in and account reads, permits closing existing trades, and blocks account purchases, opening trades, and withdrawals; it does not clear account freezes or risk locks. Approval rechecks the live balance and protected floor before deducting funds.

Admin Control's Operations area shows recently active signed wallet sessions, known all-time wallets, verified purchase sales, paid withdrawal outflow, pending withdrawals, open tickets, and pending reviews. Read-only operations refresh through the admin's bearer wallet session rather than repeatedly prompting for signatures. Financial totals use recorded purchase amounts and net payout amounts; pre-migration purchases with unknown paid amounts are excluded from sales totals and explicitly counted rather than estimated from current plan prices. Purchases record the payment asset and payer address; USDT and USDC totals are shown at face value and do not account for depegs. All-time wallet tracking is exact for new successful signed sessions after deployment; old non-profile sessions outside the 180-day IP log cannot be reconstructed. Withdrawal review includes the recipient's accounts and trades, retained login IPs, other known wallets/accounts that used those IPs, and referral purchase provenance. Historical referral records are linked only when a single matching purchase can be established; otherwise the dashboard marks the transaction as unlinked. Explorer links are shown for configured EVM networks and Solana. The public homepage activity list exposes only generic purchase and paid-reward events, without wallets, account IDs, IPs, transaction hashes, or payment amounts.

Admins can grant or revoke the fixed `SUPPORT` staff role by wallet address. A Support wallet gets a separate Support workspace containing only Tickets and Reviews. Server middleware independently restricts Support wallets to their dedicated ticket/review endpoints plus the minimum wallet-session and consent/security routes; hiding other views in the browser is not the security boundary. Ticket replies record the staff wallet, role changes are audited, and review moderation decisions are recorded. Support staff can approve or reject pending reviews but cannot access administrator dashboards, payout approvals, user/account management, or configuration endpoints.

Referral commission withdrawals record FIFO allocations to the commission records that funded each request. Admins can inspect the attributed purchase details and chain transaction for those allocations. Older withdrawals have no reliable allocation history, so their commission ledger is shown as context only and is not represented as an exact payout source.

Private account, trade, and referral reads require a wallet-signed bearer session. Tokens expire after 15 minutes of inactivity or 12 hours absolutely, are stored server-side only as SHA-256 hashes, and are removed after expiry. Session issuance is rate-limited by IP and wallet; the server also applies bounded per-IP request limits. The existing gasless wallet signature proves control of the wallet, not that the signer is human.

An IP may have at most five active wallet sessions without triggering a soft lock. A session is active for this limit while it has been used within the previous 15 minutes and has not reached its 12-hour absolute expiry. A signed session that takes the IP over that limit soft-locks only the wallet requesting that session; other wallets at the same IP are unaffected. A locked wallet cannot open new trades or access charts, but can still view account data, create support tickets, and close or flatten existing positions. The lock pauses copy-trading groups, cancels pending limit orders, and returns their reserved margin. Access restores automatically after a 15-minute security cool-off once every still-active session for that wallet has signed a fresh wallet challenge; idle sessions expire and must sign again when used. An administrator can clear a lock as a fallback. The dashboard prompts active sessions to re-sign and shows a support-ticket link.

Successful session creation records the wallet's IP address; an admin sees a warning when a login comes from an IP not seen for that wallet in the prior 180 days. IP login records are retained for 180 days and are visible only in admin user search. The application trusts only loopback proxies for `req.ip`; any deployment proxy must strip client-provided forwarding headers and set the trusted-proxy configuration deliberately. Application rate limits and wallet signatures do not stop volumetric network attacks; use an upstream CDN/WAF or equivalent edge DDoS protection.

Prices and trading remain simulated. Binance spot and perpetual prices are polled directly by the user's browser. The TradingView Lightweight Charts view shows the selected market's candles and overlays open trades for the selected account: entry, stop-loss, and take-profit price levels, plus entry markers when the trade is within the visible candle window. These are visual levels, not exchange-hosted orders. To prevent a modified client price from changing balances, the server also makes a fresh public Binance quote request only when a trade is opened or closed. This low-frequency integrity check requires no private API key. TradFi entries marked `SIMULATED` are deterministic simulated prices, not live provider data. Browser-side stop-loss/take-profit monitoring requires the terminal to remain open and wallet approval to submit the close.

This is not a financial-grade execution or risk system. A production live-trading service would need trusted market-data and order-execution infrastructure, plus an always-on server-side stop engine. `max_single_day_profit_pct` remains configurable because the repository does not define an enforcement outcome for breaching that rule.

On-chain account purchases use the EVM treasury `0x9c19edeba86b214db5b3d21da9416d5e5568365b` or the Solana treasury `A3AVrbzCYzqKEkBR7pDQGYgnAbmBF9qNMQDX84QpuRZx`. EVM wallets create and access EVM-wallet accounts, and can only buy or request withdrawals on supported EVM networks. Solana wallets create and access separate Solana-wallet accounts using Phantom or Solflare, and can only buy or request withdrawals on Solana mainnet. Both wallet types must sign when switching identities. USDT is supported on Ethereum, Base, Polygon PoS, Avalanche C-Chain, BNB Smart Chain, NullNet, and Solana. USDC is offered only on networks with Circle's native token: Ethereum (`0xA0b86991c6218b36c1d19d4a2e9eb0ce3606eb48`), Base (`0x833589fCD6eDb6E08f4c7C32D4f71b54bdA02913`), Polygon PoS (`0x3c499c542cEF5E3811e1192ce70d8cC03d5c3359`), Avalanche C-Chain (`0xB97EF9Ef8734C71904D8002F8b6Bc66Dd9c48a6E`), and Solana (`EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v`). No USDC option is shown for BNB Smart Chain or NullNet because no official Circle-issued contract is configured there. Solana USDT uses the mint `Es9vMFrzaCERmJfrF4H2FYD4KCoNkY11McCe8BenwNYB`. The application verifies finalized transactions against token mint, amount, signer, and treasury destination before provisioning accounts. Withdrawal requests record the selected network and token for manual admin payment; the admin queue displays both before approval or marking the payout paid.

On-chain purchase verification uses public RPC endpoints by default. Operators can override these server-side with `ETHEREUM_RPC_URL`, `BASE_RPC_URL`, `POLYGON_RPC_URL`, `AVALANCHE_RPC_URL`, and `SOLANA_RPC_URL`; keep provider credentials in the server environment, never in browser assets.
